# Pilot protocol

This protocol turns the first didactic pilot into a reproducible study rather than a completion-rate check. Participation must be voluntary and locally applicable consent, safeguarding, and research-ethics rules take precedence.

## Cohorts and target sample

- 10–20 newcomers with no modal-logic instruction.
- 5–10 students after one lecture on Kripke semantics.
- Students who have completed an introductory course.
- Several instructors, doctoral students, or other domain experts.

Record prior experience separately; do not rank cohorts against one another.

## Session

1. Administer the pre-test without the editor.
2. Ask the participant to complete the foundations path while thinking aloud.
3. Observe without teaching unless the participant is irretrievably blocked.
4. Administer an isomorphic post-test on paper or in a plain text form.
5. If consent and logistics permit, repeat a subset after several days or one week.

Track time to first success, edge-direction errors, local/global/frame confusion, vacuous-truth explanations, blind verification attempts, hint use, unclear affordances, graph-to-result navigation, and transfer to editor-free questions. Record task abandonment and observer interventions as well as successes.

Before recruiting participants, a facilitator should complete these release
scenarios on the deployed candidate: rename and undo a world with incident
relations; reject a duplicate/empty name; add/cancel/duplicate a relation draft;
draw both directions from arbitrary handles; delete a selected world and confirm
that no bridge appears; verify hidden derived relations and trace reveal; inspect
a frame-property witness on the map; finish a lesson with progressively revealed
hints and its optional transfer; and navigate the core route at desktop and
mobile widths with keyboard alone. Record browser, device, input hardware and
candidate commit. Any semantic mutation caused solely by a display action is a
pilot blocker.

## Pre/post-test blueprint

Use different surface details but the same constructs in both forms:

- one □ and one ◇ evaluation;
- one vacuous □ case;
- ◇◇p, □◇p, ◇□p, or □□p on a small frame;
- a local/model-global/frame-valid distinction;
- construction or identification of a countermodel;
- recognition and repair of a basic frame property.

Score semantic justification separately from the final answer. The post-test must not use this application's graph editor.

## Observation sheet

For every task record cohort, anonymous participant code, start/end time, first prediction, attempts, hints opened, success, failure category, intervention, and a short observation. Do not place names, email addresses, or sensitive free text in the application export.

## Interpretation

Report sample sizes and missing data. Compare pre/post construct scores and delayed retention where available. Satisfaction, completion rate, and locally recorded attempts are supporting signals, not proof of learning. Treat qualitative misconceptions as design evidence; do not claim causal efficacy without an appropriate study design.
